%% Glosses in a right-to-left document (decided 2026-10-10): a grid runs in
%% the direction of its EXAMPLE's language, and one that runs against the
%% document is the mirror image of \GlossRTL in a left-to-right one.  No
%% annotations: a tier that declares no language takes the example's
%% direction for the object line and the document's for the glosses.
%%
%%  - A Hebrew example: a right-to-left grid, its first column against the
%%    text edge.  XeTeX set it at the LEFT of the page, with its number in
%%    the middle: \raggedright's fill stays on the right under TeX--XeT
%%    (\__lx_gl_ragged:).  A long one is ragged on the left.
%%  - An English example (in the english environment): a left-to-right
%%    grid, a short one standing beside its number against the text edge,
%%    a long one with every line starting at the left margin -- the mirror
%%    of \GlossRTL.  Its Hebrew glosses read right to left inside their
%%    columns, each under its own word.  Its translation is in the
%%    DOCUMENT's language and direction -- Hebrew, the reader's, as
%%    \GlossRTL leaves its translation in the document's -- and stands
%%    against the text edge, reading right to left with no \texthebrew.
%%    Under XeTeX the short grid stood at the far end of its line (a \vtop's
%%    own paragraph had closed the item's segment, \lx@dir@parend), and a
%%    translation set in the example's direction came out in reverse word
%%    order.
%%
%% PLAINREF's right edge is where every example's text starts here.
