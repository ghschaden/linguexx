%% An item whose text OPENS with a literal ellipsis.  \ex is not allowed to
%% read that ellipsis as the dot of the dot syntax.
%%
%% From EISS 15 (Fusco et al.), where a continuation-style example set the
%% context in the mother item and let each sub-item resume the sentence:
%%
%%     \ea Context 1 (direct): ... based on its taste...
%%     \ea ... she doesn't consider it gluten-free.
%%     \ex ... she doesn't believe that it's gluten-free.
%%     \z
%%     \z
%%
%% \ex peeks with \@ifnextchar, which SKIPS SPACES, so "\ex ... text" and
%% "\ex. text" arrive at the peek as the same token stream and the first is
%% taken for the second.  The ellipsis is prose here; nothing in the source
%% asks for the dot syntax, and langsci-gb4e -- whose front-end this is, and
%% whose \ex never peeked -- set the item without complaint.
%%
%% The damage is not confined to the item.  Under [lazy,langsci] the peek
%% consumes one dot and hands the rest to the dot syntax inside an \ea
%% example, so the case
%%   - loses a dot, rendering ".." where the source wrote "...",
%%   - raises the one-syntax-per-example error,
%%   - promotes the sub-item to a NEW top-level example, leaving \z with no
%%     level to close and the list open at \end{document}.
%% Hence the label assertion: one example number, not two, is what separates
%% a fix at the peek from one that only patches the rendering of the dots.
%%
%% Under [langsci] alone (the Language Science Press book setting, without
%% [lazy]) the same peek reaches a dot-syntax branch that is not installed,
%% and the document dies with "The dot syntax (\ex.) is not available under
%% [gb4e]" -- one bug, two faces.
%%
%% \ea is not affected and is here as the control: it opens a level and
%% never peeks for a period, so its own leading ellipsis has always been
%% prose.  A fix that makes \ex agree with it is the whole of what is
%% wanted.
\input{_preamble-langsci}
\begin{document}

\ea ELLIPCTX Kim doesn't know the ingredients that went into this cake, but
    based on its taste...
  \ea ... ELLIPSUBA she doesn't consider it gluten-free.
  \ex ... ELLIPSUBB she doesn't believe that it's gluten-free.
  \z
\z

\end{document}
