%% The third cell of the \verb matrix: the braced judgment form.
%%
%% \ex[j]{text} takes its body as a MACRO ARGUMENT, so the tokens are read
%% -- with the catcodes in force at the \ex -- before the body is ever
%% used, and no code of linguexx's sees them first.  (The collected
%% dot-syntax body had the same predicament until 2026-10-04, when its
%% collector learned to read inline verbatim itself: verb-dot.tex.)  The
%% "_" arrives at the typesetter as a subscript, "Missing $ inserted", on
%% all three engines.
%%
%% It is worth pinning separately BECAUSE the route differs.  This case
%% lives under exe, where verb.tex proves \verb does work with an unbraced
%% \ex on the very same line of the very same environment -- so what fails
%% here is the braced form alone, not the environment, and a change that
%% made the argument form verbatim-safe would show up as this case going
%% green rather than as nothing at all.  The control that says so: with the
%% \verb removed, this exact file compiles clean on every engine.
%%
%% The manual (§ Notes and limitations) states the whole matrix and calls
%% this cell the exception; verb.tex pins the environment syntax and
%% verb-dot.tex the dot syntax, where it works too.
\input{_preamble-gb4e}

\end{exe}
