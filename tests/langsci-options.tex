%% [manualexewidth] and [lowerpenalty], the same two knobs as
%% langsci-exewidth.tex with the options given.  Neither case means anything
%% alone: with the option code deleted, this one still shows an unwidened box
%% and that one still shows the kernel penalty.  It is the DIFFERENCE between
%% the two that is the assertion, which is why they carry the same sentinels.
%%
%% [lowerpenalty] is asserted through the penalty it sets and not through a
%% page break, deliberately.  Whether a batch breaks across a page at -51 or
%% only at -1000 is a knife-edge that depends on the font, the page and how
%% full it already is; a case built on it would pass or fail for reasons that
%% have nothing to do with the option.  So what is checked is the mechanism,
%% and this comment is here to say that the outcome is not.
