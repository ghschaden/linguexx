%% \CommonGloss in a right-to-left document: the table is laid out as an
%% ordinary gloss in the same place is -- its frame (letter, grid,
%% annotation column) in the document's direction, its columns in the
%% example's.  Each table is measured against ordinary glossed sub-examples
%% with the same words, set in the same file, so nothing here hard-codes a
%% length.
%%
%% The defect this was written for: the table asked for no direction at
%% all.  Under XeLaTeX every table was set left to right at the LEFT
%% margin, letters on the wrong side and the annotation under the head
%% line; under LuaLaTeX a Hebrew table came out right by accident (the row
%% box took the paragraph's direction) and an English one in this Hebrew
%% document had its columns reversed, EA3 EA2 EA1.
%%
%% (1) a Hebrew table, judgment, gap, annotation; (2) the same rows as
%%     ordinary glossed sub-examples: the table's gloss words, mark and
%%     annotation must stand where (2)'s do.
%% (3) an English table in \begin{english}: letters on the right, columns
%%     from the left; (4) the same row a as an ordinary gloss -- its columns
%%     are the table's, since row b's words and the glosses are no wider.
%%     The annotation and mark of (3) are in the frame, so at (1)'s x.
%% (5) no head line: the number stands on row a's line.
%%
%% Latin sentinels throughout where a position is read: pdftotext reports
%% Hebrew in visual order under XeTeX.  The ordinary English gloss (4) has
%% no annotation on purpose -- in a crossed ordinary gloss it follows the
%% grid, which the table, whose annotation column is part of its frame,
%% does not (see the comment above \__lx_cg_dir_decide:).
\input{_preamble-rtlmain}
