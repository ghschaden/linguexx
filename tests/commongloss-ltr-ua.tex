%% \CommonGloss for a Hebrew example in an English document: the frame left
%% to right as every example's here, the columns right to left, as an
%% ordinary gloss of such an example has them (\GlossRTL's layout, without
%% \GlossRTL).  Before, the table asked for no direction and set the
%% columns left to right on both engines.
%%
%% (1) an English table, the reference; (2) the same table for a Hebrew
%%     example, with the same gloss words: its columns must run from the
%%     right (lg1 rightmost) and fill the same span (2)'s grid does, its mark
%%     hang at the letter where (1)'s does (provisional for a right-to-left
%%     grid, doc/DEFERRED-DECISIONS.md), and its annotation stand in (1)'s
%%     column, which an ordinary right-to-left gloss refuses (rtl-annot)
%%     and a table can give, its column being part of its frame.
%% No head line on (2): under LuaLaTeX polyglossia reverses Latin text in a
%% hebrew environment, and under XeLaTeX a tagged head line in a
%% right-to-left example fails veraPDF with or without linguexx's table.
%%
%% (3) a table under \GlossRTL whose tiers declare no language: such a
%%     tier is left to right, and under XeLaTeX its Hebrew words read
%%     "ץע" for "עץ" until a right-to-left word carried /ActualText
%%     whatever its tier.
%%
%% PDF/UA-2, so veraPDF runs on it: under XeTeX every cell is an L segment
%% holding its marked content, or the R segment of the grid reverses the
%% BDC/EMC pairs with the text.  Three passes (PASSES).
\input{_preamble-ua-polyglossia}
\ex. CLREF
\ag. LA1 LA2 LA3 \exannot{ANN}\\
\bg. *LA2 {} LA3\\
\CommonGloss lg1 lg2 lg3\\

\begin{hebrew}

\bg. *ד {} ו\\
\CommonGloss lg1 lg2 lg3\\
\end{hebrew}
